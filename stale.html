Translations that may be out of date

A translation is made from a source term at a moment in time. When that source term is later rewritten, the translation stays exactly as it was, still counted as translated, with nothing to say it now describes something that has changed. Wikidata cannot see this, because a term carries no link to the term it was translated from. The revision history can: it records which term, in which language, was last touched when.

Checking reads one revision history per property, so this asks Wikidata a few hundred times at most and keeps what it reads for a day. Start with the most used properties: those are the ones whose wording has been argued over, and so the ones most likely to have moved.

How this is worked out

Wikibase writes a structured summary for every term it changes, naming the kind of term and the language. Reading a property's history backwards therefore gives the last date each term in each language was set. A translation dated before the last change to the term it was made from may no longer match it. Some edits change terms without saying which — a bulk import, an undo — and where one of those falls after the source change, no verdict is given rather than a guess. A finding is a lead, not an error: the source edit may have been a typo fix, which is why each one can be opened to show what actually changed.
